# Lake of Nur underwater board suite, T5

Three opaque ImageGen masters were generated through foreground `tools/run_imagegen.py` calls, one each for `WATER_FLOOR`, `WATER_WALL`, and `WATER_DOOR`. Complete prompts, native/style references, call logs, original output paths and SHA256 receipts are in `art/production/handoffs/underwater-t5-v1/`. No repairs, threshold changes or waivers. The masters retain the native RGB opaque output (terrain-floor rule), 1254×1254; `export.py` deterministically produces 42 RGB 128px runtime images (21 parity pairs). Door opening is a floor plus fixed jambs; existing approved stone stair silhouettes are tinted blue and composited on the underwater floor. The native air and door objects remain untouched.

Reviewed [48px](review/contact-48.png), [64px](review/contact-64.png), and [96px](review/contact-96.png) contacts against the existing stone and sand suites. Wall masonry is a full blocking mass; floor is a brighter continuous plane; closed and open doors differ by a solid leaf versus a visible passage; stair direction differs by rise versus recess. The 21 exported parity pairs have minimum mean-luminance separation **18.38%**, above the unchanged 10% gate; wall-15 is over 30% darker than floor. [Raw metrics](review/pixel-metrics.json) and [export hashes](export-manifest.json) are machine checkable. Shader-on rendered-frame and remembered-view checks are in `evidence/t5-20260929/`.

Limitations: the master textures themselves are not mathematically seamless; the 128px cells intentionally retain visible board-cell seams. The rendered review confirms walls remain legible under the game's FOV lighting, including memory. Air bubbles and Sher'Tul gates use native art because their callbacks and story cues are outside the static contract.
